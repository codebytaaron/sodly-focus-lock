Self-imposed. Opt-in. For your own calls.

Lock yourself in before the meeting pulls you out.

Sodly is a menu-bar app that puts up a persistent reminder and gently nags you back when you drift to Slack, Twitter, or fifteen open tabs during a call. You start it. You can always stop it. It just makes drifting take a little more deliberate effort.

How it works

1

Start Focus Lock

Click the menu-bar icon before your call, pick a duration (15/25/45/60 min), and Sodly puts a small always-on-top banner at the top of your screen.

2

Get nudged, not blocked

If you switch to an app that isn't your call (Zoom, Meet, Teams, FaceTime), Sodly notices, counts it, and brings the reminder back to the front.

3

Give up on purpose

Timer runs out on its own, or you can hold the button for 9 full seconds to give up early. No hidden bypass, no accidental exit either — and macOS's own Force Quit always works as a safety valve.

⚠️ Read this before you install

Sodly is a focus aid, not a parental-control or enterprise-lockdown tool. It's built to be honest about what it can and can't do on macOS without special system entitlements:

  • It cannot truly prevent you from switching apps. macOS doesn't let ordinary apps block Cmd+Tab or the Dock without Accessibility permissions and much deeper OS integration. Sodly detects and reacts — it doesn't lock the OS.
  • It works by watching focus changes (Electron focus/blur events plus periodic active-window polling) and popping the reminder back in front of you.
  • It always has an escape hatch. A deliberate hold-to-give-up control is a design choice, not a bug, and macOS's own Force Quit (Cmd+Opt+Esc) always works by design — an inescapable lock on your own machine would be dangerous, not helpful. Force-quitting or rebooting leaves no background process running.
  • It's for locking your own device, voluntarily. There is no remote control, no admin mode, no way to lock someone else's computer.
